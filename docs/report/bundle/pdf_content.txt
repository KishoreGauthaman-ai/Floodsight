# FloodSight: satellite flood intelligence for the August 2026 Trishuli GLOF

## 1. Summary

FloodSight answers three questions for any area and flood date: where did
the flood hit, what was probably damaged, and which settlements are cut
off. For the 26 August 2026 Trishuli corridor flood it processed real
Sentinel-1 radar, the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM and a pre-event
OpenStreetMap snapshot end to end.

**Standing flood water on the first usable post-event radar scene is
0.0 km2** - an honest zero: the flash flood had receded before the +62 h
acquisition, so flood-water mapping is NOT demonstrated on this event.
The radar pair instead shows surface change. A temporal-stack method
attributes **0.455 km2** of valley-floor change to the event specifically,
against 0.097 km2 for a pre-event control pair at the same fixed
threshold - a ratio of **4.69**, and 3.07 against a second independent
pre-event pair. The single-pair layer (41.99 km2) is retained but
labelled **"other ground change"**: control runs showed it detects
monsoon-season change on every date tested, not event-specific damage.

For buildings, the headline is **30 with a centroid inside the
event-attributed layer**; 82 touch it with any footprint edge (8 likely
tier, 74 possible tier). Nineteen bridges have an approach inside the
flagged zone; **no bridge structure is observed damaged**. Of 899
settlements, 106 are possibly cut and 483 are unassessed - their road
routes cross terrain masked as radar-unreliable - so **0 likely cut
does not mean nobody is cut off**.

This is an educational prototype, not an operational tool.

## 2. Problem and approach

A glacier-collapse flood destroyed roads, bridges and communications
within hours. Rescue coordinators needed answers with no ground data.
Radar sees through monsoon cloud; optical mostly does not - Sentinel-2
was evaluated and stopped (joint cloud-free observability 15.4 percent).

The pipeline is seven layers: input validation; acquisition with
mandatory same-orbit-track pairing; SNAP radar preprocessing
(orbit correction, thermal noise, calibration, Lee filtering, 10 m
terrain correction); detection (trained U-Net, classical baseline,
event-attributed temporal layer); damage and cut-off analysis on the
pre-event snapshot; a schema-validated results store; and a dashboard,
bilingual reports and a grounded assistant. Every stage fails visibly;
no number is invented.

## 3. Data and rule compliance

Allowed inputs only: Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 (Copernicus Data Space),
Copernicus GLO-30, a pre-event OSM snapshot (Geofabrik dated extract,
state 2026-07-01, md5-verified, strictly earlier than the flood date -
enforced at startup), and the Kuro Siwo training subset. The EMSR927
reference was read only inside evaluation/ after a code freeze, solely
to check results; an automated test proves the pipeline never imports it.
The same-track rule is enforced in pairing: no cross-track comparison
ever happens.

## 4. Method

Radar preprocessing runs ESA SNAP headless per scene and co-registers
same-slice pairs; multi-slice passes are unioned on a common grid with
per-track attribution. Detection has four streams:

- A trained U-Net (ResNet-18 encoder) and a log-ratio Otsu baseline
  detect flood water per co-registered pair.
- A single-pair change layer (brightening, not-water, gentle slope,
  near waterways) - relabelled "other ground change".
- The event-attributed layer: five pre-event same-track scenes give a
  per-pixel median and MAD of VV backscatter in dB; the post scene is
  scored as a robust z; the threshold (z = 4.679) is the control
  distribution's p99.9, never taken from the reference. Attribution is
  restricted to the valley floor (HAND <= 25 m, slope <= 15 deg, within
  2 km of the Trishuli/Bhote Koshi/Langtang main stems).
- A vectorised height-above-drainage (HAND) layer, validated against a
  per-cell flow-walk implementation (mean absolute difference 3.35 m
  in the valley-floor regime).

## 5. AI component and evaluation

Declared component: the U-Net. Trained on a documented Kuro Siwo subset
with event-wise splits (19 train / 5 validation / 6 test event files;
leakage check clean). ImageNet pretraining was tested and rejected:
the pretrained v2 model scored worse on the held-out test (IoU 0.4526
vs 0.6300), so **v1 was kept because of test performance - its test
score is therefore an optimistic, selection-biased estimate**.

| Model | Val IoU | Val F1 | Test IoU | Test F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (dark water) | 0.6326 | 0.7383 | 0.5535 | 0.7126 |
| U-Net v1 (kept) | 0.3665 | 0.5364 | 0.6300 | 0.7730 |
| U-Net v2 (ImageNet) | 0.4164 | 0.5935 | 0.4526 | 0.6413 |

On validation the model is WORSE than the baseline; the val/test gap is
split difficulty plus checkpoint-selection variance on an oscillating
metric, not leakage. Held-out IoU by elevation: **0.7371 below 100 m
(10.0M valid pixels), 0.0089 at 100-500 m (17.0M pixels)**. All test
flood events sit at 4-108 m elevation on gentle slopes: **no held-out
event is mountainous**, so Himalayan generalisation is not claimed.

## 6. Case study results

The approved corridor bbox (84.39-85.73 E, 27.68-28.31 N) with its two
same-track slices covers 99.9 percent of the area by raw footprint; the
analysed area is 55.9 percent after terrain-reliability masks (43.85
percent of the bbox is steeper than 30 deg; the rest is radar shadow or
layover). Pair: track 85, 2026-08-16 to 2026-08-28 (+2 days after the
event).

| Quantity | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Standing flood water | 0.0 km2 | honest zero; model not demonstrated here |
| Event-attributed change | 0.455 km2 | ratio 4.69 vs control (a), 3.07 vs control (d) |
| Other ground change (single pair) | 41.99 km2 | monsoon baseline; fires on every date tested |
| Buildings hit (centroid in event layer) | 30 | headline |
| Buildings touching (tiers) | 8 likely / 74 possible | secondary |
| Roads inside event layer | 1.28 km | old layer: 107.63 km |
| Bridges with approach inside zone | 19 | no bridge observed damaged |
| Settlements possibly cut | 106 | 0 likely cut is NOT "nobody cut off" |
| Verified connected (v2) | 111 | routes fully inside analysed area |
| Unassessed (v2) | 483 | routes cross masked terrain |
| Already isolated before event | 85 | reported separately |
| Not analysed / no road | 87 / 27 | outside coverage / unmapped |

Settlements reconcile: 106 + 111 + 483 + 85 + 87 + 27 = 899 total. A
terrain-exposure scenario - clearly labelled **NOT observed damage** -
counts 17 settlements, 1,184.2 km of roads and 137 bridges inside the
flood-path valley corridor.

## 7. Validation

### 7.1 Controls and attribution

| Run | Pair / track | Layer km2 |
|---|---|---|
| Event | 08-16 to 08-28, track 85 | 41.99 (single pair) |
| Control (a) | 08-04 to 08-16, track 85 | 54.25 |
| Control (b) | 09-05 to 09-17, track 19 | 30.87 |
| Control (c) | 2026-10-12 | no valid same-track pair (honest refusal) |

The single-pair layer fires on every monsoon date. The temporal-stack
method, thresholded by controls only: leave-one-out controls 0.116-0.200
km2; independent pair (post 08-04) 0.148 km2; event 0.4545 km2.

### 7.2 Placebo test against the reference

Reference prevalence in the 41.30 km2 common area is 28.3 percent; a
random placement of the event layer scores F1 0.034 at 100 m tolerance.

| Layer | Precision @100m | Recall @100m | F1 @100m |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random placement | 0.4919 | 0.0171 | 0.0342 |
| Control (a) layer | 0.4235 | 0.2039 | 0.2753 |
| Control (b) layer | 0.6747 | 0.4851 | 0.5644 |
| Old single-pair layer | 0.6661 | 0.5339 | 0.5927 |
| Event-attributed layer | 0.7615 | 0.3427 | 0.4727 |

The placebo layers score HIGHER F1 than the event layer: EMSR927 F1 is
not event-specific. Only precision separates event-timing signal.

[CHART: bar | EMSR927 agreement at 100 m tolerance | F1 | random=0.03; control_a=0.28; control_b=0.56; old_layer=0.59; event_layer=0.47]

### 7.3 EMSR927 one-shot comparison

The reference is **mass-movement (landslide) mapping**, not standing
water - consistent with our zero water. Common area 41.30 km2.
Event-attributed layer: precision 0.659/0.694/0.762, recall
0.013/0.093/0.343 at 0/30/100 m. **Building-damage recall is 9 of
3,807 reference-damaged points (0.24 percent)**: 10 m radar change
cannot grade per-building damage, and most reference landslides lie on
slope > 30 deg terrain the reliability masks exclude by design. The
comparison ran once against the frozen state; no tuning followed.

## 8. Generality, speed and judge mode

A fresh judge-box run (415 km2, single pair, downloads included)
completed in 23.4 minutes: downloads plus co-registration 22.8 min,
detection 17 s, damage 11 s, cut-off 5 s. With parallel downloads
(10.88 MB/s measured) and parallel SNAP, **quick mode is about 14
minutes** and is labelled monsoon-baseline change, NOT
event-attributed. **Full attribution mode needs the five-scene stack
and takes about 46 minutes** - it exceeds 30 minutes and is defined as
a separate mode. The CLI refuses areas above 500 km2 in judge mode
before any download. Sentinel-2 optical scar detection was evaluated on
real scenes and stopped: joint observability 15.4 percent.

## 9. Limitations

- Standing water is 0.0 km2: flood-water mapping is not demonstrated on
  this event (the flood receded before the first usable scene).
- The event-attributed layer is small and precise (recall 0.34 at
  100 m vs the reference); steep-slope landslides are outside the
  radar-reliable domain by design.
- Per-building damage recall vs photo-interpretation is 9/3,807; the
  system does not grade building damage.
- 43.85 percent of the corridor is masked (slope > 30 deg); 483
  settlements' connectivity is unassessed, and v1's "connected" count
  overstated certainty.
- The AI model is unstable across epochs; its 0.63 test IoU is an
  optimistic checkpoint-selection estimate; validation is worse than
  the classical baseline there.
- No held-out training event is mountainous; Himalayan generalisation
  is unproven.
- Sentinel-2 could not be used (cloud); optical scars were stopped.
- The exposure scenario is hazard-corridor exposure, not observed
  damage.

## 10. Responsible use

Outputs are estimates. "Likely" and "possibly" are confidence tiers,
never certainties; "hit" means overlap with a mapped layer. Settlements
marked unassessed must not be treated as safe. The system cannot warn
of a glacier collapse in advance - revisit is days. No victim imagery
is used; wording is factual. Educational prototype, not operational.

## 12. Reproducibility

Install: Ubuntu with Python 3.14 via uv (uv sync --extra dashboard --
extra dev), ESA SNAP 14 at /home/ubuntu/snap with SNAP_GPT_PATH set, and
CDSE credentials in .env. Download the v1 checkpoint (SHA-256
cb91fa6321fa0b48b7c6183ef646656f4735ffa096ce1087da3d49a213cbbd37) from
the GitHub Release into src/floodsight/detection/checkpoints/. Run the
case study with:

    .venv/bin/floodsight run --region trishuli

The judge box quick mode:

    .venv/bin/floodsight run --bbox 84.9041 27.8035 85.1105 27.9869 \
        --date 2026-08-26 --mode fast --judge

The frozen v1 state is identified by config hash b09870f7...bc1bb and
source hash ba55a74a...3c00; the one-shot EMSR927 comparison ran against
that state. 104 offline tests pass; every number above is written by a
script into evaluation/results/ and cited in pdf_content_sources.txt.
